# Your nightly job search

A guide to what this does, what to expect from it, and everything you can
change.

## What this is

Every night, while you are asleep, a search runs for you. It goes through a list
of employers, reads what they have posted, and decides which of those jobs are
worth your morning. What it finds is waiting for you when you wake up.

It is not a job board and it does not send you alerts. Nothing arrives during
the day, nothing interrupts you, and there is no feed to keep checking. One run,
one morning, one list.

It knows what you are looking for in your own words, not as a set of filters.
You tell it the kind of work you want, where you will take a job, and what you
will not go below, and it reads each posting against that the way a person
would. A posting with the right title that describes the wrong job gets turned
away, and you are told why.

## Getting in

You start from a link someone sends you. It works once, it expires, and it
makes exactly one account.

Open it, pick a name and a password, and you land on a short form:

- **What to look for.** The kind of work, in your own words. "Senior or Staff
  backend engineer" is the right shape of answer. This is the one people get
  wrong: it means the kind of *role*, not the companies you like. Naming
  employers here sends the search looking for a city called Amazon.
- **Where to search.** Every area the search should cover, one per entry. Name
  all of it, not only the part you would prefer.
- **Which of those you would prefer.** Rank them. The ones you rank are always
  searched, whatever else you listed.
- **Anywhere you cannot take a job.** Ruled out before anything else.
- **Anything else.** Free text for what a list cannot say, like "open to
  relocating for the right team". The search reads it as context, not as a rule.
- **Companies to leave alone.** Leads from these never reach your tracker.
- **Your resume.** Attach a PDF, a Word `.docx`, a `.txt` or a `.md`, or paste
  the text instead. More than one is fine.

You can also say how you want to be referred to. Skip it and the search uses
they/them.

Press send and your tracker appears immediately. It will be empty. That is
expected, and the next section is why.

## The first night, and what to expect

**Nothing happens until a night passes.** When you finish the form your tracker
appears straight away, but it stays empty until the run that night. If you sign
up on Tuesday afternoon, Wednesday morning is your first real list. This is the
most common surprise, and nothing is wrong when it happens.

**The first morning can be thin.** Some people wake up to a dozen leads and some
to two. Neither tells you much yet.

**Before a search has ever run, its tab says "No run recorded yet" and the
Overview says it is waiting on the first recorded run.** That is what a
brand-new account looks like, and it is not a fault. If you add a second search
later, you will see it again on that search's tab while the first one shows a
real date.

**After that, each tab carries the date it last ran**, and the Overview says
whether every search is reporting on schedule. This is the first thing to look
at when a morning seems wrong, because it tells "nothing was found" apart from
"nothing ran". The tab also carries a badge counting the leads nobody has looked
at yet, which is a smaller number than the Open list: once you mark something
Reviewing it leaves the badge and stays on the list.

**Give it a week before you judge it.** The search works through employers in a
rotation rather than re-reading the same ones every night, so your first few
mornings are a sample of the list rather than the whole of it.

**If a morning is empty, read the Screened tab before changing anything.** Empty
with nothing screened means the night found little. Empty with forty screened
means it found plenty and your own rules turned all of it away. Those are
different problems with different fixes.

## Your tabs

Across the top, in this order: **Overview**, **Applications**, **All leads**,
then one tab per search, then **Screened**. All of them can be renamed, not only
the searches.

Every tab has a search box, can be seen as a grid or in detail view, and can be
exported to CSV, either what is currently shown or everything.

### Overview

A summary across every search: how many positions were found, how many leads are
still open, how many applications you have sent, and how many of those have had
a reply, with **Momentum** for this week so far. Below that, where your leads
are concentrated, your pipeline by stage, how long replies take, and which
applications have gone quiet.

**Which searches pay off** is the one to read when you are wondering whether a
search is worth keeping: a row per search, ending in the rate between what it
found and what you applied to. A search that finds a great deal and converts
none of it is telling you something a count of leads will not.

Every figure opens the rows behind it, so a number you do not believe is one
click from the list it came from.

### Applications

The **Applications** tab is the record of what you sent and where it went.

Each one moves through stages: **To Apply**, **Applied**, **Recruiter Screen**,
**Tech Screen**, **Onsite / Loop**, **Offer**, **Rejected**, **Withdrawn**.

Each stage stamps its own date once and keeps it. So an application rejected
after a tech screen still records that it got to a tech screen, which is what
makes the pipeline on your Overview worth reading.

Chips narrow the list: **Live**, **Applied**, **In conversation**, **Rejected**
and **All**. The tab opens on Live, which is everything you have not been
rejected from and have not withdrawn. **In conversation** covers the three
interview stages and Offer.

One thing to know before you go looking for something: an application you
**withdrew** appears under **All** and nowhere else. It has not been deleted.

Sorting offers **Newest applied**, **Oldest applied**, **Longest waiting**,
**Location A-Z** and **Company A-Z**. Reach for Longest waiting when the
Overview tells you something has gone quiet.

Two ways to add something that did not come from a lead:

- **Add from link.** Paste a job posting's address and it reads the posting
  overnight and fills in the company, title, location and pay. If it could not
  read the posting, the row says so and waits for you to fill it in.
- **Type it in.** An empty row you fill yourself.

Each application also has fields only you fill in, for a referral, the pay on
offer, the team and your own notes. The search never touches those.

### All leads

Every search's leads pooled into one list. It behaves exactly like a search's
own tab, described next, and it is where the Overview's figures open when you
click them, so a number you followed lands here rather than on one search's
tab.

### A tab for each search

This is where a night's work arrives: the jobs that search found and has not
been told what to do with.

The chips are **Open**, **New**, **Reviewing**, **Not a fit** and **All**. It
opens on Open, which covers New and Reviewing together, so a job you marked
Reviewing is still in front of you rather than filed away.

Rows arrive newest first. Sorting offers **Newest found**, **Priority**,
**Oldest found**, **Location A-Z** and **Company A-Z**. Priority orders by how
well each job's location matches the places you ranked, and is the only one that
pushes anything you marked **Not a fit** to the bottom. Your choice of sort
sticks in the browser you made it in, so it can differ between your laptop and
your phone.

**What a row tells you.** The company, the title, where the job is, and what it
pays when the posting says. Opening a row shows the rest, including the sentence
the search wrote about why this one fits what you asked for. That sentence is
the most useful thing on the page, because it tells you what the search thought
you meant. You can add a note to any lead, and the link always goes to the
posting itself.

**Acting on one.** A lead is **New** until you do something with it. Mark it
**Reviewing** while you think, **Applied** when you send something, or **Not a
fit** to set it aside without losing it. Marking it Applied moves it into your
applications, keeping what the search already knew so you are not retyping.

**Not a fit** is worth using rather than ignoring a row. It keeps the job on
record and, under the Priority sort, drops it to the bottom, so your open list
stays the things actually waiting on you. You can also remove a posting
outright. Removals are yours, so they never appear in the Screened tab as
something your settings did.

### Screened: what did not arrive, and why

This is the part no job board gives you.

Every night, most of what the search reads gets turned away. The **Screened**
tab is that pile, with a reason on each one. It exists so that the cost of your
own settings is visible to you rather than silent.

Five of the reasons are your rules at work:

- **Location** - outside the areas you gave it.
- **Level** - too junior or too senior for what you asked for.
- **Bad fit** - the wrong kind of work despite a plausible title.
- **Pay** - the whole stated range sits below your floor. A range that reaches
  your floor stays, and so does a posting that names no pay at all.
- **Contract** - not permanent, when you asked for permanent.

The rest are facts about the posting rather than decisions of yours: **Already
seen**, **Gone before you saw it**, **Taken down after you saw it**, **Other**
for a rejection none of the named reasons describes, and **Not grouped** for
older rows from before runs recorded which rule turned a posting away.

The distinction is the point. A night that met a dead link and a duplicate did
not reject anything on your behalf. A night that screened forty jobs for **Pay**
is telling you something about your floor.

You can look at today, the last 7 days, the last 30, the last 90, or everything,
and narrow to one reason at a time. "Today" means the day of the most recent
run, so on a morning after a night that did not run, it still shows that run's
day.

Each of the five reasons that are your rules tells you which setting caused it
and where to change it, so a pile you did not expect leads straight to the thing
that made it.

Nothing here is deleted. Older postings stop appearing in the window you are
looking at, but they are still remembered, and they still stop a search bringing
you the same job twice.

## My account

- **General.** What your tracker is called, how you want to be referred to, the
  companies you never want to see, your password, and signing out.
- **Locations.** The three lists, edited a place at a time: where to search,
  which of those you prefer and in what order, and anywhere you cannot take a
  job. Plus the free-text note for what a list cannot say.
- **Resumes.** Attach another, or choose which one a search reads.

### Retuning a search

The **Searches** section holds, for each search, **what roles it should look
for**, **what makes a posting worth keeping** and **what rules a posting out**:
the same three questions the setup form asked, in the same words. It also holds
the pay floor you will not go below, which belongs to that one search rather
than to you.

Reach for these when the Screened tab says your rules are off. A pile under
**Level** means the roles line is drawn wrongly; a pile under **Bad fit** means
what counts as worth keeping needs saying differently.

You can also pause a search. It stops running, keeps everything it has found,
and its tab says **Paused** where the run date would be, so you can tell it from
one that has simply stopped working.

**When a change takes effect** depends on what it is. What your tracker is
called, how you are referred to, and the companies you have ruled out are true
the moment you save. What a search reads - its resume, its place lists, its pay
floor, the description of what it is looking for - is picked up by that night's
run, so it changes tomorrow's results rather than today's. Pausing is recorded
at once and stops the next run.

Ruling out a company stops it reaching you from then on. Leads already on your
board stay where they are.

## What it will not do

Worth knowing up front, so none of this disappoints you later.

**It does not apply for you.** It finds, reads and sorts. Every application is
yours to send, and the tracker records what you did rather than doing it.

**It does not watch the whole internet.** It works from a list of employers that
every search on the service shares. That list is not fixed: a search that turns
up a promising company adds it to the rotation, so the list grows on its own.
But a company nothing has found yet is one no search is looking at, and the page
has no button for adding one after setup. Ask whoever invited you.

**It skips postings that have been open a long time.** A job that has sat open
past the point of being worth an evening is not brought to you at all, and does
not appear in your screened list either.

**The judgements are made by a machine reading text, and some will be wrong in
ways a person's would not be.** A posting it turned away might have been worth
your time, and a lead it brought you might be nothing. The Screened tab exists
so the first kind of mistake is visible to you rather than silent, and reading
it occasionally is the best way to know whether to trust the rest.
